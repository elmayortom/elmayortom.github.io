It all started because of the music

RoleReporter, photographer & editorial writer PublicationVarious online media outlets

I got my first real job as a reporter for the biggest music magazine in Guadalajara, the city where I lived at the time. I'd been a music fan my whole life, and had spent years wishing for a chance to work on something I actually loved. This felt like exactly that — my dream job, handed to me.

I picked up my first camera at a concert. Turns out shooting a show is a brutal setting for a beginner: everything moves, the light is barely there, and flash is pretty much banned. I didn't know what I was doing. I got better.

In just 2015 alone, I went to around 100 concerts, wrote hundreds of articles, and interviewed a solid mix of international and local bands. Music journalism let me combine two of my biggest passions — telling stories, and never missing a good show.

I reviewed one of the most important albums in Calamaro's career. If Alta Suciedad (1997) was a summary of his life, Honestidad Brutal (1999) is the full autobiography, told in first person — a stream of consciousness where every version of him shows up at once: the sailor, the lovesick dog, the cynic, the bullfighter, the naive romantic, the bohemian bourgeois. A double album, long enough — almost two hours of music, 37 songs — to show a complex artist wearing many different faces. Calamaro always tells the truth here. That much honesty hurts. A fun one to review.

Al Jourgensen is one of the most charismatic frontmen in modern rock and metal. In this phoner, we talked about his upcoming projects and the passing of his bandmate Mike Scaccia. He talks like a pirate — a raspy voice, bursts of laughter, brutally honest, and fully committed to his role as Satan's envoy. He's a sharp conversationalist who loves provoking whoever's on the other end of the line. We spoke about the end of Ministry, his distrust of journalists, and more than a little nonsense along the way. The full interview is transcribed here, unedited — it's the only way to capture the actual rhythm of the conversation.

A photo story from Miércoles Alternativo, Guadalajara's recurring night dedicated to alternative music. I liked this show so much — Esteman and Pedrina y Rio, two Colombian acts with a small but devoted following in Mexico — that I decided to skip the usual review and tell the whole story through pictures instead. From Pedrina's pastel-laced Converse to Esteman's mid-show costume change into a South American alebrije, this is less a concert recap and more a visual diary of one very good night at Teatro Vivian Blumenthal.
