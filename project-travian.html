Is it possible to revive a 20-year-old gaming community on Facebook?

RoleCommunity Manager ClientTravian Games PlatformFacebook Result+7% engagement

The short answer is yes.

Travian: Legends is a browser-based game pushing past its 20th year — still loved by a community scattered across the world, though its glory days are long behind it.

When I took over as community manager of its Facebook page, it had a million followers and hadn't posted anything that felt alive in years. The page wasn't dead. It was just... asleep.

So I started experimenting. Memes built around the community's own inside jokes. YouTube Shorts the page had never tried before, written and edited from scratch. New recurring series to give people a reason to check back. Alongside all that, I ran the day-to-day: launch campaigns, engagement posts, actually talking to players instead of just posting at them.

A few months in, engagement was up 3%.

If you know, you know: Travian memes

Travian: Legends is a deeply complex, real-time strategy game — which can be intimidating for anyone new. So I started making memes, poking a little fun at how serious the game usually takes itself.

The unexpected part? It didn't just attract new players. It brought back people who had churned out long ago, pulling them right back into the community.

Introducing... Travian Minutes

One of the formats I introduced was Travian Minutes — game updates announced in under sixty seconds, reel-style, built for how people actually watch content today. No long patch notes, no walls of text. Just the update, fast and to the point.

I was behind every part of it: the idea, the script, the voice, the edit.

A whole new content grid

Beyond memes and Minutes, the entire content grid on the Facebook page got a full revamp — visuals, tone, and content types included. Community events, posts designed to spark real conversation, exclusive content to help newer players catch up, and launch campaigns to build hype around what was coming next.
